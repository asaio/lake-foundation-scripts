My Journey

From Individual Contributor to Junior Engineering Manager

Exploring growth, feedback, and leadership evolution.

Individual Contributor

- Focused on technical excellence and delivery.
- Held myself to high standards.
- Received feedback: sometimes applied those standards too rigidly to others.

Tech Lead of a Squad

- Applied situational leadership to adapt to each engineer.
- Mentored two engineers from junior to senior, building confidence and performance.
- Supported diverse contributions through one-on-one talks and study plans.
- Feedback: supportive, approachable, balanced expectations.

Transition to Junior Engineering Manager

- Guided by Start With Why: enabling others to succeed.
- Using First 90 Days STARS framework.
- Sustaining Success: protect consistent delivery record.
- Realignment: address low NPS, adopt new technologies, build accountability.
- Develop people actively during work hours, provide actionable feedback.

Leadership vs. Management

Leadership Management
Inspires vision and purpose Defines processes and strategies
Focuses on WHY and direction Focuses on HOW and execution
Motivates and empowers people Ensures accountability and delivery
Influence without formal authority Formal responsibility for people and outcomes
Drives change through inspiration Sustains success through structure

Staff Engineer vs. Junior Manager

Staff Engineer Junior Engineering Manager
Leads through technical influence Leads through people development
Defines architecture and technical vision Defines processes and team strategies
Multiplies impact via systems and code Multiplies impact via individuals and teams
Focus on depth of expertise Focus on breadth of growth and accountability
Influence is primarily technical Influence is both technical and organizational

Why Management Made Sense

- I sought to lead through influence but also desired to define processes and strategies.
- Mentored engineers from junior to senior, building confidence and performance.
- Helped diverse contributors thrive through one-on-one talks and study plans.
- With strong delivery but low NPS, realignment was needed.
- Management allows me to multiply my role through team and individual development, ensuring accountability and growth.

Conflict Management Skills

Internal: Investigated concerns of probable sexism, confirmed patterns, coached the individual on active listening and explicit communication, set monthly follow‑ups, and improved team dynamics.

External: Realigned expectations with a supplier rushing us into alpha testing. Clarified our SLOs and negotiated realistic deadlines based on minimal product testing and use‑case knowledge.

Cross‑Functional: Resolved misunderstanding with the cloud foundation team about a supposed security exception. Explained calmly, clarified history, and secured restoration of network capability due to business impact.

Outcome: Demonstrated impartial investigation, clear communication, and conflict resolution across internal, external, and cross‑functional contexts.

First 90 Days Virtuous Cycles

- Diagnose the Situation: Don’t rush to act; understand context and history.
- Establish Relationships: Build trust with stakeholders, clients, and peers.
- Seek Quick Wins: Identify achievable goals to gain credibility early.
- Gain Credibility: Deliver results and show consistency.
- Virtuous Cycle: Each success builds momentum, reinforcing trust and enabling larger changes.
- Outcome: Transition smoothly into management, balancing sustaining success with realignment.

Vision Forward

- Multiply impact through people.
- Balance high standards with empathy.
- Create an environment of growth, accountability, and innovation.
- Deliver not just consistently, but with excellence and satisfaction.
